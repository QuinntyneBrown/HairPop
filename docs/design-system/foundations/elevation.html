Foundation

Elevation

Elevation says what sits above what: cards rest on the page, menus and toasts float, dialogs sit over a scrim. Four shadows and nine named layers cover every case.

  • Overview
  • Shadows
  • Surface hierarchy
  • Z-order
  • Usage
  • Tokens

Overview

Elevation has two parts: a shadow that suggests height, and a surface colour that confirms it. In light, surfaces are the same white and shadows do the work; in dark, shadows are hard to see, so higher surfaces are also lighter grey. Stacking order is a separate decision made with --z-* layers; no component sets a raw z-index.

Shadows are built from --shadow-color (an RGB triplet) with alpha, so the dark theme swaps the colour and raises the alpha without new shadow shapes. --shadow-focus draws a two-ring halo (surface gap, then ring) for elements where an outline would be clipped.

In the dark theme, canvas (grey 950) < surface (grey 900) < raised (grey 800): a dialog reads as raised even with no visible shadow.

Usage

  • Use the shadow that matches the level, never a custom one. Two shadows in the mocks are not elevation: the invalid input's inner ring and the selected card's 1px ring are borders drawn with box-shadow and stay in the component.
  • A surface floats only when it is temporary (menus, toasts, dialogs). Permanent content rests at shadow 1.
  • Pick the z-layer by role. If two things collide, the higher role wins; do not add 1 to a token. (The mock chrome bar's calc(var(--z-tooltip) + 10) is documentation chrome, not product UI.)
  • Sticky and fixed elements never cover the focused element (WCAG 2.4.11): the top bar sets scroll-padding-top, and toasts dock away from form actions.
