Content

HairPop talks like a trusted braider: warm, plain and confident. It says what will happen, what it costs and what is still to be confirmed.

Overview

People come to HairPop to make a personal decision that takes hours and costs real money. The words help them feel sure: they name the braider, the style, the time and the price range, and they are honest about what is not yet confirmed.

We areWe are notExample
WarmGushing"Find a style that feels like you", not "Discover your most amazing look ever!"
PlainTechnical"We couldn't load braiders", not "Request failed (503)".
ConfidentPushy"Nia will confirm the final price and time", not "Book now before it's gone".
HonestVague"Estimated price: $180–$240", not "Great prices".

Request versus appointment

The most important word choice in the product. Until the braider accepts, it is a request; afterwards it is an appointment. Prices before confirmation are estimates.

Casing and punctuation

  • Sentence case everywhere: headings, buttons, labels, menu items, badges in source text ("Awaiting reply"; CSS uppercases badges and eyebrows visually).
  • No full stop on headings, labels, buttons or single-sentence toasts titles; full stops on body sentences.
  • Curly apostrophes and quotes (’ “ ”); an en dash for ranges (4–7 hours); a middle dot with spaces to separate facts (Toronto West · 4.9 from 86 reviews).
  • No exclamation marks except in a genuine celebration, and never in errors.

Error messages

Formula: what happened + what is safe + what to do next. Never blame the person, never show a code without words.

Enter an email address like name@example.com.

We couldn’t save this change. Your details remain here. Please try again.
An inline error, a page error and an action toast written with the formula.
HTML
<p class="field__error">Enter an email address like name@example.com.</p>
<div class="alert alert--danger" role="alert"><div><strong>We couldn’t save this change.</strong> Your details remain here. Please try again.</div></div>
  • Field errors name the fix, not the rule that failed: "Choose a rating from 1 to 5", not "Invalid input".
  • Page errors reassure first: "We couldn't load braiders. Check your connection and try again. Your details are safe."
  • Offline says what still works: "You're offline. Saved appointment details remain available. Requests need a connection."
  • Server errors say what did not happen: "Your booking request has not been resent."

Usage

  • Use people's first names once a relationship exists: "Your appointment with Nia". Use full names on first mention and in lists: "Nia Okafor".
  • Address the customer as "you"; HairPop is "we" only when HairPop itself acts ("We couldn't load…", "We'll send a secure link").
  • Keep headings under about eight words and buttons under four.
  • Describe hair respectfully and specifically ("medium knotless braids", "shoulder length"); avoid "good hair", "manageable" and similar judgements.
  • Do not change the label of a busy button to "Working…"; keep the verb and announce progress separately.

Accessibility

  • Link and button text makes sense out of context (WCAG 2.4.4): "Explore Passion twists", "Retry reviews".
  • An accessible name begins with the visible words (2.5.3).
  • Alt text describes the hair in the photo, not the file: "Medium knotless braids shown from the back and side".
  • Write for reading level grade 7–8; short sentences help everyone, including people reading in a second language.
  • Do not rely on direction or shape alone: "the purple button on the right" means nothing to a screen-reader user.
