Pattern

Content and tone

HairPop sounds like a knowledgeable friend who books braiders: warm, specific and honest about what is confirmed and what is not.

WCAG 2.2 AA

Overview

Customers choose someone to spend five hours with their hair. Copy builds that confidence by being specific (Nia, Sat 24 Oct, $180–$240), honest (an estimate is an estimate; a request is not an appointment), and calm (failures say what is safe before what went wrong). The content foundation has the full style rules; this pattern shows how they combine on screens.

  • Warm, not cute. "Find a style that feels like you." Not "Slay your next look!!"
  • Specific, not generic. "Nia will reply with the final time and price." Not "We’ll be in touch."
  • Honest about status. "Awaiting Nia’s reply", never "Booked" before she accepts.
  • Second person for Maya, first names for braiders.

Structure

One screen, every content rule: eyebrow, a title that speaks to Maya, a subtitle of facts, an honest status, a next step named with a verb.

Responsive

  • Write once for 360px: titles of eight words or fewer, button labels of three or four words, subtitles that can wrap at a " · " separator.
  • Never truncate a person’s name, a price or a date; let it wrap.
  • Do not write different copy per breakpoint; shorten the source instead.

Accessibility

  • Link and button text make sense alone: "View Nia’s profile", "Cancel request" (2.4.4).
  • Icon-only controls get a verb-and-object label: "Close dialog", "Dismiss notification".
  • A visible label is part of the accessible name (2.5.3): the link that reads "Updates 2" is named "Updates 2, 2 unread".
  • Status words appear as text, not colour alone: "Awaiting reply" on the badge.
  • Plain language: short sentences, common words, no idioms that do not translate.
  • Decorative glyphs (★, →, ·) are carried by the surrounding text or aria-hidden; a rating on its own reads "4.9 out of 5 stars" through visually hidden text.
