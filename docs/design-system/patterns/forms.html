Pattern

Forms

Forms are where Maya commits: a booking request, a review, her contact details. Every HairPop form is one column, labelled above, validated on submit, and recoverable without retyping.

Overview

A HairPop form is a card titled with what it collects, holding one column of form fields, an optional estimate or note, and one primary button that names the outcome ("Send request", "Post review", "Save changes"). The mocks use this shape for booking, care and review, account settings and every auth screen.

  • One column. Fields stack in reading order. Two columns appear only for short related pairs at 768px and up (.form-row).
  • Label above; help and error below the control. Help stays visible while the error shows beneath it. Placeholders are hints, never labels.
  • Validate when a changed field is left, or on submit, then re-validate on input. No errors while Maya is still typing a field for the first time, and none for a field she only passed through.
  • Nothing is lost. Errors, offline and failure states keep every value she entered.

Structure

Card title, fields, a note that sets expectations, then the action. The primary button is full width on phones and sits at the end of the form from 768px.

States

Invalid: summary linked to fields

On submit, show a danger alert as the form's first child that counts the problems in its title and lists each field as a link reading "{label}: {the field's own error}", move focus to the summary (a role="group" with tabindex="-1", named by its title), mark each field aria-invalid="true" and tie its message with aria-describedby, error first.

Busy keeps the label and focus; success replaces the form with a next step; offline keeps the button enabled and explains on activation.
  • Submitting: only the pressed button gets aria-busy="true"; fields stay editable and nothing is disabled. Progress text goes to a role="status" region.
  • Failed (server error): keep the form, add a danger alert above it ("We couldn’t send your request. Your details are still here. Try again."), and keep focus on the button.
  • Success: replace the form with a success empty state that states what happens next and links to it. Forms that stay on screen (account settings) use a success toast instead.
  • Destructive confirmation: never a form. See dialogs and overlays.

Responsive

  • Under 768px: one column, the panel fills the container, the primary button is full width and first in .form-actions.
  • From 768px: .form-row pairs short related fields (email and phone, city and postal code); the primary returns to its natural width at the end.
  • Inputs stay 40px tall with 16px text at every width, which also stops iOS from zooming on focus.
  • The summary is not sticky; it scrolls into view because it receives focus.

Accessibility

  • Every input has a visible <label for>. Help and error text are siblings of the input, linked with aria-describedby (error first, then help, then the counter); never inside the <label>, which would read the error as part of the name.
  • novalidate on the form; validation messages are ours, written to name the fix (3.3.1, 3.3.3).
  • On a failed submit, focus moves to the summary (programmatically, not with autofocus), and each summary link moves focus to its field.
  • autocomplete on name, email, phone and password fields (1.3.5); paste and password managers are always allowed (3.3.8).
  • Information Maya already gave (service, time chosen on Nia’s profile) is pre-filled (3.3.7).
  • Busy and offline states never disable the submit button, so it stays focusable and explains itself.

Content

  • Card titles say what is collected: "Your details", "Your review", "Contact details".
  • Labels are nouns in sentence case: "Preferred time", "Your email". Questions only when the answer is a choice: "How did it go?"
  • Error formula: what to do + why or an example. "Enter an email address like name@example.com." Not "Invalid input".
  • The summary title counts the problems: "Check 1 field before sending", "Check 2 fields before saving".
  • Set expectations beside the button when the outcome is not final: "Nia confirms the final price and time."
